independent candidates; no selection of their outputs becomes an algorithm.

Before any candidate implementation: concise hypothesis, pseudocode,
self-critique and a cheap falsification experiment in its track note. A's
existing ownership specification and 043 protocol already provide these.
B and C are both new constructors. Shared pilot changes have one owner.

Exploration requires isolated dependencies, targeted mechanism/deadline tests,
independent embedding validation, and every failure and full runtime retained.
Reuse the audited pilot, process supervision and validators. Exhaustive tests
and publication-grade replay are reserved for promising mechanisms or changed
shared correctness infrastructure. Each candidate uses structural input only,
one fixed general policy and ideal Z12. No MM/busclique dependency, global IP,
cached competitor input, family dispatch or portfolio. Candidate/MM timing
pairs run on the same host, in separate processes. Runs survive SSH loss;
loss of observation never triggers a second launch.

At each screen milestone report hypothesis, success/failure counts, quality on
valid pairs, all timing, lessons and one next decision. Failed construction is
not evidence of infeasibility. Development results are exploratory; promotion
requires broader development coverage and later untouched-instance evaluation.

Self-critique 1: small panels can favor a mechanism accidentally. Freeze each
panel before outcomes and retain all observations; screen mechanisms, not
claims of superiority. Expand only after informative evidence.

Self-critique 2: concurrency can cause shared-code races and consume effort in
infrastructure. Assign distinct module/host ownership, one pilot owner, and
reuse existing checks. A failed hypothesis should promptly lead to a recorded
lesson and a revised or replacement candidate, not an unlimited audit.
