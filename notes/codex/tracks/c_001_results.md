Three specific defects in the initial search policy explain useful next work. First, its forced unmatched pairing can combine nonadjacent source aggregates during a matching level, even when the original source is a path. This increases boundary demands unnecessarily; the failed path split already had seven required child-to-current-region contacts. Second, selecting six seeds before checking whether each disconnects its donor wastes every attempt on articulation vertices in the grid failure. Third, proportional target-region size is an aspiration that the code incorrectly makes mandatory: on the complete input, a 245/360-site child partition had every required contact and exceeded both 56-site minimum capacities, yet the attempt still failed because child A had not reached its 303-site target size. These are general structural search issues, not family-specific exceptions.

The broader risk remains after these corrections: preserving one coarse contact does not reserve suitable independent endpoints for future splits. The failed path/ER/SBM attempts often absorbed most of a region while a required external contact remained inaccessible under donor connectivity/contact preservation. Simply increasing the seed or wall budget does not address that limitation.

Next decision: retain this failed version unchanged, and test one C002 revision that keeps matched source aggregates connected, filters impossible seeds before the six-search cap, and allows a fully certified capacity-feasible split when additional balancing transfers are blocked. Keep the exact same eight-input panel, seed, deadline and fresh-MM pairing. Do not promote the mechanism unless it first establishes useful construction success.
