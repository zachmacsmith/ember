# Track B: next placement/feasibility design pass

2026-09-08. No new implementation or solver run in this design pass. B004's fixed reinsertion schedule is stopped. It reduces one bipartite output by 107 sites but worsens three other successful outputs and spends five million unsuccessful repair scans on regular. Local immediate Q and one remaining free port do not characterize future feasible contacts.

Three general options:

1. **Propagate domains, then relax singleton assumptions.** Give each unplaced logical vertex a domain of currently free physical sites touching all its placed neighbors. For still-singleton variables, remove sites that have no adjacent support in a neighboring variable's domain. Choose a variable with the smallest surviving domain, with degree and deterministic ties. Try a bounded number of private singleton assignments with forward propagation. When these assumptions fail, explicitly promote a variable to connected-chain construction and relax its singleton constraints. This tests compatibility of unplaced logical neighbors, which B003's independent domain-size ranking never checked. Critique: singleton infeasibility is not minor-embedding infeasibility. Rebuilding domains after promotion or old-chain growth is necessary; retaining stale deletions would be unsound. All high-degree vertices can require promotion immediately, so this alone may reproduce the dense-case limitations of the current tree primitive. Arc consistency and smallest-domain ordering are conventional constraint-programming methods, not a novelty claim.

2. **Reserve distinct future contact capacity while chains grow.** Maintain assignments from unfinished logical contact demands to available physical boundary sites; repair those assignments by augmenting paths before committing growth. This addresses competition for the same apparent free ports. Critique: one distinct port per future neighbor is only a sufficient local reservation and can overconstrain valid minors, since extending through one port can create several future contacts. A local matching still ignores route connectivity and globally incompatible edge demands. Relaxing that reservation requires a principled growth model, otherwise this becomes a more expensive version of B001's rejected demand count. A decisive toy would need a reservation conflict that predicts an actual failed extension, rather than merely a missing matching under an unnecessarily strong restriction.

3. **Grow all chain regions simultaneously with temporary contact deficits.** Start from distinct singleton seeds, keep every chain connected/disjoint, and repeatedly grow or transfer boundary sites to reduce missing source contacts. Retain an exact missing-contact objective before Q; bounded local exchange can release ownership instead of inserting vertices irreversibly. Critique: a deficit-decreasing move may not exist without a temporary increase, and empty-state random seeds can place endpoints very far apart. This overlaps the general region/quotient research in Track C and existing negotiated-routing ideas. A separate B prototype would duplicate that track without a concrete new mechanism.

Select option 1 for the next cheap mechanism test, with explicit limits on the conclusion. It changes the placement strategy rather than increasing the local reinsertion allowance. It is one evolving partial minor: domains represent provisional singleton assumptions, not a collection of competing embeddings. Promoting a variable or rebuilding domains is relaxation within that state; it does not restart construction or select among algorithms. The connected-tree routine remains a routing primitive inside this construction rule.

Before implementation, the concrete protocol must settle: the propagation work allowance and incomplete-queue semantics; which variable is promoted on an empty domain; the domain reset after any relaxed constraint or changed old chain; the finite value order; and how deadline failures retain the last valid partial minor. No deletion from a propagated domain may be reused after its assumptions change unless justified anew. A partial propagation pass may leave unsupported sites but must never claim arc consistency.

Cheap falsifier: construct a tiny original-graph state in which all individual unplaced domains are nonempty but an unsupported adjacent-variable pair makes one tempting singleton assignment impossible. The exact implemented propagation must exclude that assignment, retain a feasible alternative, and produce a valid minor without changing the supplied graph. Also test a graph that requires a multi-qubit chain to ensure promotion is an actual construction mechanism, not an implicit failure. If these fail, do not launch the development panel. If they pass, a globally fixed seed-0 screen on the same nine inputs can ask whether earlier constraint information saves final Q at roughly MM-scale runtime. Dense failures remain part of the conclusion; sparse improvement alone is not the project goal.

Self-critique of selecting this option: it has the clearest cheap falsifier and is distinct from the other active tracks, but those are engineering reasons rather than evidence that it will beat MM. The degree-driven promotion caveat is substantial. A result improving sparse graphs while leaving both complete graphs failed would justify only further mechanism study, not a general constructor claim. Do not silently switch to B003 on inputs where propagation is unhelpful.
