## Structural argument

Immediately before eliminating `v`, every original edge from `v` to a surviving vertex remains in the filled working graph: elimination adds edges between survivors but never removes their existing edges. At this step the filled degree of `v` is at most three. Reverse insertion has exactly those survivors already placed, so `|N_original(v) ∩ placed| ≤ 3`. This remains true across disconnected components and isolates.

This says nothing about the number of **unplaced** original neighbors, distinct free target sites, or chain length. A retained high-degree hub still needs growth; preserve the inherited original-demand frontier protection and all its failure reporting. The original induced core may be disconnected, have low-degree vertices, or be edgeless despite a nontrivial filled core. Do not re-reduce it: that would change the specified core vertex selection.

## Cheap falsifier and self-critique

Before any broader screen, check the small structural witness obtained by subdividing one edge `a–b` of K5 with a new vertex `v`. Filled elimination removes degree-two `v` and leaves K5; the original induced core is K5 minus `a–b`. With the six-vertex original graph itself as target, its five singleton core vertices and the remaining singleton `v` give a hand-checkable valid lift. No five-site K5 exists in that target, so filled-core construction requires at least six sites. This demonstrates differing contact requirements, **not** that the heuristic will choose the useful original-core placement. A supplied core stub can test exact core input and reverse insertion without invoking a second constructor.

Reuse the already fixed star, wheel and degree-two subdivision reach cases, plus the nested-fill journal fixture. Require original-valid complete outputs and at-most-three placed original neighbors at every insertion; retain every failure under unchanged caps. The star has no fill, so a nonbinding unchanged-path check should preserve its choices. Verify final/prefix original adjacency, deadline rollback and absence of synthetic contacts in core arguments and builder requirements. These are small mechanism checks, not a new input selection.

Removing contact obligations can weaken the spatial guidance that fill supplied. Fewer core edges may place future neighbors far apart, increase routing length, intensify port contention, or worsen coverage even if core Q falls. Original-only pending demands are less restrictive and can produce different irreversible growth decisions. Edgeless-core placement may be cheaper but less informed. Total quality must therefore be measured after lifting; core Q alone cannot promote this variant. The eventual comparison, if authorized, is one fixed050 ancestor versus this one modified constructor on the same inputs and deadlines, retaining failures and all timing. No novelty or guaranteed lifting claim is made.
